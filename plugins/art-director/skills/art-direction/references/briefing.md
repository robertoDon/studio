# Art-direction briefing

Seven questions. Answer yourself whatever the project X-ray already answered, and mark it **(read)**. Ask only what is missing and changes the choice, up to 3 at a time, always with options.

## 1. What does the product show?

The "subject" of the screen decides a lot about the school.

| Mostly shows | Pulls toward |
|---|---|
| Numbers, tables, status (dashboard, CRM, finance) | Swiss, Utilitarian, Dark Tech, Refined Minimalism |
| Long text, stories, news | Editorial, Japanese, Refined Minimalism |
| Physical product with photos (fashion, food, home) | Quiet Luxury, Editorial, Handcrafted, Scandinavian |
| People and community (social, events) | Pop/Memphis, Maximalist, Bento |
| Body and performance (training, sport) | Sport, Dark Tech |
| Serious service (health, government, banking, legal) | Trusted Corporate, Swiss |
| Creative or technical tool (dev, AI, audio) | Dark Tech, Tactile, Utilitarian, Spatial Glass |
| Learning and childhood | Clay 3D, Pop/Memphis, Material |

## 2. Who is the audience?

- **Age:** child · teen/Gen Z · 25–40 · 40–60 · 60+.
- **Context of use:** on the go on a phone · at a computer for hours · once a month · in public/on the street.
- **Technical level:** layperson · regular user · professional/power user.
- **Relationship:** B2C (chooses alone, decides fast) · B2B (buys with others, wants trust).

Quick rules: 60+ needs type ≥ 18 px and high contrast. Power users handle density and want shortcuts. Gen Z forgives boldness and punishes the generic. Street use needs large targets and strong contrast.

## 3. What image does it want to project?

Ask for **up to 2 adjectives** from this list (or map what the person said onto one of them):

`trustworthy` · `modern` · `cool` · `chic` · `fun` · `welcoming` · `handcrafted` · `technical` · `bold` · `calm` · `energetic` · `affordable-premium` · `youthful` · `natural` · `smart`

If the person only says "pretty" or "modern", ask: "Modern like Linear (dark, technical), like Apple (clean, airy) or like Duolingo (color, energy)?" Showing an example settles more than debating adjectives.

## 4. Where does it live?

Web app · mobile app (native or PWA) · landing page · corporate website · e-commerce · slides (deck, pitch, presentation) · one-off piece (post). It can be more than one. The landing page can be bolder than the app; the app rules day to day.

## 5. Who are the competitors and what do they look like?

List 2–3. The question is: do we want to **look like the sector** (trust) or **look different from the sector** (stand out)? That decides between the safe route and the bold one.

## 6. What is already fixed?

Logo, brand color, font, house yardstick, DESIGN.md, recorded decisions. What is fixed is not up for debate; the school adapts.

## 7. What must not happen?

E.g. "must not look childish", "must not look like a bank", "no mascot", "no dark mode". This becomes the "Forbidden" section of the DESIGN.md.

## When nobody answers

State your assumptions and move on: "I assumed a 25–40 audience, B2C, phone use and a modern + trustworthy image. If that changes, the school changes." A written assumption beats stalled work.
